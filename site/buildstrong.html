BuildStrong

The business runs. It runs through you.

You built it, and every hard call still stops at your desk. Not because the work is difficult, but because there is nobody else who can make those calls the way you would. BuildStrong is the work of building somebody who can.

The Problem

You have written the processes down. They still ring you.

Everything that still reaches you is a judgement call. The client threatening to leave. The two bad options. The quote that does not fit the price list. None of it can be documented, because it was never the same situation twice.

That is the part a system cannot hold. It has to be built in a person, by exposure and correction, over time.

What usually happens instead

You have tried to hire it four times.

Different names, different years, the same role. One you did not offer to. One turned you down. One came and left within a year. The conclusion each time was about the market, that the good ones are expensive and stay where they are.

Every one of them was fine at week six. Something happens around month three, which is when a senior person stops learning how things are done here and starts deciding things.

Hiring is a way of buying somebody who already knows how you decide. That is not on a CV and it does not arrive during a probation period.

The part nobody says

Not everyone is good at developing others.

Being excellent at something makes you worse at teaching it, not better. When you have done a thing ten thousand times you stop seeing the steps, you see the answer. So watching somebody work it out slowly is genuinely difficult, and you step in, every time with good intentions.

Developing people is a specialism with its own techniques and its own discipline. Nobody ever taught it to you. You were taught the work, you got good at the work, and one day you had staff.

That is a gap rather than a flaw, and gaps close.

The work

The owner learns to let go. The successor learns to step up.

Two halves, and they run together rather than in sequence.

i.

Plan and strategise with the owner

What the successor needs to be able to do, and what he cannot do yet. Then what gets handed over and in what order, what you keep deliberately rather than by habit, and what happens the first time he gets one wrong. That last one is the step everybody skips and it decides whether the rest holds.

ii.

Build and stretch the successor

Exposure, correction, proof. Live decisions worked through at the board, scenario testing, off stage practice for on stage performance, and the work of merging his own values with the role so that what comes out is his rather than an imitation of you.

We start with assessments that make each of you legible to the other, and there are regular check ins with you throughout, because the hardest part of this is usually not his development.

Two phases

First somebody who can hold the place. Then somebody you could leave in charge.

Holding the place means the business runs for a fortnight without you and nothing needs deciding twice. That takes eighteen months to two years, and it is the part most owners actually want.

What comes after that is a different conversation, and it is one worth having deliberately rather than drifting into.

When this is not the answer

Sometimes it is genuinely process.

If nothing has ever been written down and you are answering the same question forty times a week, a person will not fix that. If the work is repeatable with clear rules, that should be a system rather than somebody's judgement. And if there is nobody in the business who could plausibly hold the place, this is a hiring conversation rather than this one.

I would rather say so in the first hour than six months in.
