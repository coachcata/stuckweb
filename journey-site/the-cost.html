A short essay

So you've decided this is what the job costs.

At some point, without a meeting or a memo, you settled it: this is what senior roles are like. The hours colonise the evenings, the thinking gets done on the move or not at all, and the tiredness stops being an event and becomes a climate. You watched it happen to the people above you on the way up. Now it is happening to you, and you have filed it under the cost of operating at this level.

You are not wrong about the pattern. It is real, it is close to universal at your altitude, and anyone who suggests it can be waved away with an app and an earlier bedtime has not done your job. The load is genuine. The decisions are genuinely heavy. There is no version of this work that feels light.

But notice the word underneath that belief: price. A price is fixed by someone else; you either pay it or leave the shop. Calling this a price settles the question of whether it could be otherwise, which is convenient, because a settled question needs no examining. And it is worth saying plainly: the version of you who settled it did so quickly, under load, some years ago, and has not been back to check the working since.

The person who decided this couldn't change was busy at the time.

There is one more thing worth knowing about the pattern, and it can be said without drama. It does not hold still. Left alone, it has a direction of travel. The decisions do not get smaller next year. The thinking time does not grow back on its own. The instinct you are running on was built from thinking you did some time ago, and it is being spent a little faster than it is being replaced. Nothing dramatic happens; things simply get decided, one by one, slightly below the standard you would hold them to if there were room. You may already know this. It may be why the subject changes when it comes up.

No one is going to argue you out of a belief you have lived inside this long, and a web page certainly won't. The only honest offer is a way to check the working yourself, quietly, without telling anyone or joining anything.

If the belief survives the hour, keep it. At least it will be a decision then, rather than a habit.
