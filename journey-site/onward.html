For the ones who've sat at the board

You did the work. It's still working.

If you're reading this, then at some point you cleared an hour, brought something live and difficult, and thought it through at the board. Much of what went up there will have faded by now; that is what boards are for. What tends to last is the other thing: the way you catch yourself asking the second question, naming the second option before settling for the obvious one, giving the decision the hour it deserves instead of the walk between meetings. That was always yours. The sessions just gave it room, and then you kept the room.

The door works the way it always did.

There is no programme to re-join and no cadence to keep. When something goes live again, a new remit, a decision that will not land, a question that deserves better than the cracks of a week, an hour at the board is a booking away, not a negotiation. Come back the way you came the first time: with whatever is actually live.

The ongoing space

The Considered Leader, weekly.

Between sessions, the thinking continues in writing: a short weekly letter, one idea at a time, for exactly the kind of mind that sits at the board. If it isn't already arriving, leave an address.

Noted. The next letter will come to you.

And one thing more, said once: if a peer comes to mind who would value this kind of thinking, an introduction is always welcome.

Don't get stuck.
