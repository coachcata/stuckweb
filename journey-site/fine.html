A short essay

Fine. Just busy.

You would say you're fine, and you would mean it. The work is getting done. The numbers are where they need to be, or close enough to defend. People still bring you the hard problems, which is its own kind of compliment. When someone asks how you are, busy does the job. It has been doing the job for a while now.

Here is the part that doesn't get said. You cannot remember the last time you thought about one thing for a whole hour. Not reacted, not decided, not cleared. Thought. The week is a wall of other people's questions, and you have become very good at answering them. Decisions that used to get an evening now get the walk between meetings. Mostly they land, because you have been doing this long enough for your instincts to be good. But somewhere underneath, quietly, you have noticed that you are running on instinct nearly all of the time.

It shows in small ways, none of them worth raising with anyone. The question about next year keeps rolling forward from one week's list to the next; it has been doing that for a month now, maybe longer. You read two pages of something worthwhile and put it down. Sunday evening arrives with a low hum you have stopped mentioning, because nothing is wrong, exactly, and you would struggle to name what you'd change.

None of this is a crisis, and this page isn't going to pretend it is. It is a pattern, and the pattern has a shape.

Depletion that arrives this way doesn't announce itself. It borrows against the part of you that does the thinking, a little more each quarter, while the part of you that performs keeps performing. The performance is why nobody notices. It is also why you don't.

If none of that landed, close the page with a clear conscience; it wasn't written for you. If some of it did, there is one thing here worth taking, and it isn't an argument.

Worth an hour, sometime

An Hour at the Board, On Your Own.

A structured hour for thinking one thing through properly, alone, with a pen and any surface that will take ink. There is no one to talk to, nothing to join, and nothing that follows. It exists because the hour it protects has usually been gone for years before anyone says so.

People tend to arrive at it the way you may have arrived at this page: someone who knows them passed it along without comment.
