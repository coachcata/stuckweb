An Hour at the Board, On Your Own.

One question. One surface. One protected hour. Nothing to join, and nothing that follows.

Thought held in the head behaves differently from thought made visible. The same problem, written out on a board, a window, or the back of a large envelope, starts to show its joints: the connections appear, the weighting becomes possible, and the piece you have been circling for weeks turns out to be answerable in an afternoon. Most senior people know this. Very few have an hour in which to use it.

This is a written guide to running that hour alone. It covers how to choose the one question that deserves it, how to get the first honest sentence up, what to do when the thinking stalls at minute twenty, and how to recognise the moment you are actually done. It is the working method of a whiteboard session, folded down so one person can run it without company.

It asks for nothing except the hour, a pen, and a door that shuts.

What it is not.

It is not a taster, a webinar, or the first step of a sequence. Taking it does not put you in a funnel, because there isn't one. Your address is used once, to send the hour, and then it is left alone unless you ask for something more.

How to receive it

Leave an address. It arrives as a single page.

Done. The hour is on its way. Take it when the week allows, and take all of it.

Why free? Because the hour is the point. If it hands you back one piece of clear thinking, it has done its job, whether or not you and the person who wrote it ever meet.
