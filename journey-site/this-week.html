For a week like this one

Something has put it in front of you.

Until recently the question was theoretical, and you were managing. Then something changed shape. A new appointment, bigger than the last one and watched more closely. A team you inherited rather than built, with dynamics you can feel but cannot yet name. A decision that has sat unlanded for weeks while everything downstream of it queues. Or the other kind of change: a role ending, and a next chapter that is suddenly yours to define, with nothing pre-drawn on the page.

Whichever it is, the effect is the same. The thinking you had been postponing has stopped being postponable. The stakes have turned personal, and the fifteen-minute gaps where you normally do your deciding are visibly not enough for this one. You may have noticed yourself rehearsing the problem in odd places: driving, half-listening on calls, at three in the morning with a clarity that is gone by breakfast.

Two things are true at this moment, and they pull in opposite directions. It is genuinely urgent. And almost every mistake available to you from here comes from moving before the thinking is done. So the useful thing is not reassurance, and it is not speed. It is room, structure, and, if you want it, another mind.
